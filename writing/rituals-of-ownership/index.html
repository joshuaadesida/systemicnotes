Rituals of Ownership

The expectation for women to take a man's last name is the same logic that allowed the Cornell 7 to happen.

Before the world found out about the horrific, and vile gang rape at Cornell University (shame on you), I was having a conversation with my friend about feminism, which a a core tenet of my world view. He mentioned when broaching this topic with other men, the totally of the response he seen can be distilled to:"Feminists haven't made enough progress convincing non-feminist men to join their cause because they make men feel bad for being men." I have also seen this sentiment grow, in connection to "don't be afraid to be a proud cis white male," like they're the ones under attack.

Trying to understand this, he floated the idea—playing devil's advocate—that if you want a man to take a stand, you have to make the cause "palatable" and have to incentivize it, offer him emotional and moral buy-in, because standing up for women carries a risk to his social standing or safety. There shouldn't have to be a "buy-in" to do what is the bare minimum.

I reject the premise entirely, thinking that men need some sort of reward or a warm welcome to do the bare minimum of protecting women from violence is a deeply transactional worldview, and it's the exact same capitalist logic that got us here in the first place. It also take away agency from men who see the inequality, making it seem like they’re pandering to woman.

If you have to imagine it was your mother or daughter to care, you don't really care—you only care when it could affect you. But if that's what it takes to see women as people, that's the bare minimum. I'll admit that was what my thinking was until I learned more, and it almost happened to someone I cared about. Now you don't have to sell me shit, because I have a worldview I am grounded in.

If you want to understand why the Cornell 7 felt comfortable enough to text each other "free pussy" in a group chat, you don't have to look much further than the most normalized, "traditional" rituals of patriarchy we still celebrate today.

Marriage, Property, and the Logic of the Cornell 7

We can't talk about extreme violence against women without talking about the everyday systems that normalize it. The reason why those frat rapist felt entitles to a woman’s body is because we still live in a society that treats women as property to be acquired, transferred, and owned. (I am pro choice for this reason)

Just take a look at the rituals and traditions we all still partake in. The one that comes top of mind is the expectation that a woman takes a man's last name, asking a father for his daughter's hand in marriage. These are unquestioned in todays society and just normal. As Marx and Engels pointed out, marriage has historically been a way to subjugate women and trap wealth. Women were literally seen as property to be passed from father to husband. The wife took the husband's name because she was absorbed into his lineage, his estate, his identity.

When you grow up in a society that still clings to these rituals of ownership—where a woman's body is seen as something to be "given away" by a father, or "claimed" by a husband—you shouldn't be surprised when young men feel entitled to women's bodies on a college campus. (yes it is that deep). Cornell isn’t and isolated case, it has happened millions of times before in a system that still whispers to men: you are the default, you are the owner, and women are the property. The same rituals that treat women as property is the same logic that says a man needs a reward to defend her, as if protecting a woman entitles you to her body

It's like speaking up against Israel's genocide. I speak up because it's unconscionable to my worldview. I'm not gonna wait till it's socially acceptable, where the trade-off isn't as bad as being pro-Palestine a couple of days after Oct 7. Fuck the social standing. I'm standing on principles. And in the future, everyone would say they've always been against this.

The Responsibility Is on Us

When I see videos of people shitting on men for being a certain way, I don't get defensive. I don't become a keyboard warrior because I know they're not talking about me. I know its not all men, but you best believe I ain’t arguing, and understand why the bear was chosen. I do my part in defending women, and educating other men, and there is a lot more work to be done. Those who feel the urge to defend men and type some shit are the ones who it's talking about.

We live in a patriarchal society and the byproduct of that is men only listen to other men, and even if feminists were saying it in a palatable way, most men wouldn't listen because it's not coming from an authority figure which we have automatically assumed to be a man. My point in saying all of this is that men have to make an outsized effort to fight against gendered violence and speak to other men because we unfortunately still live in a patriarchal society. The responsibility is on us, because woman are tired of this.

False accusations?

It’s a fear I had when I was younger, due to racialized cases, but the answer is not to build our movements around the fear of false accusations. The vast majority of the time, women are telling the truth. The reason we doubt them isn't because they're lying—it's because we live in a society that has always protected men who do this. When you have the President of the United States, every politician, and these billionaire pedophilic capitalist elite ruining everything; shit is fucked up and it creates an environment where people just let it go.

We have to be honest about the complexities of domination and normalized violence. Sometimes people don't even realize they've crossed lines. My friend later said he's had conversations with women who describe experiences they see no issue with, but when they tell him the story, there's no doubt in his mind he raped her. It's been so normalized to them that they don't see it, and just because they may be okay with it doesn't mean they're not a victim.

When I hear a woman say a man did something, my default is to believe her. Not because I think all men are guilty, but because I know how rarely women are actually heard. And when the accusation is against someone I know, I don't need a court to tell me how to move. I can choose to distance myself. I can choose to say: I don't fuck with that behaviour. If you do that or joke like that, you're out of my life. That's accountability and community standard we should have been holding all along. We need to bring that same energy to the informal spaces between men, because we want to build a world where women are safe and men are actually held responsible by the people around them.

The System is the Root

The same logic that says a woman's body is property says a Black body is criminal. The same logic that says a father gives his daughter away says a colonizer takes the land and calls it discovery. The same logic that says a man owns his wife says a CEO owns his workers, a state owns its prisoners, an empire owns its colonies.

These aren't separate issues. It's one system: capitalism. It needs hierarchy to function. It needs a bottom to extract from. It needs racism to justify stolen labour. It needs patriarchy to justify unpaid domestic work. It needs imperialism to justify bombing hospitals in Gaza and calling it self-defence. It needs the extraction of Congo, the genocide in Sudan. It needs prisons to disappear the people it discards. It needs all of it.

When we say "men need to hold other men accountable," we're not saying that because we think male violence is the only problem. We're saying it because male violence is a symptom of the same disease that gave us the Cornell 7, the genocide in Palestine, the police killing Black people, and the billionaires hoarding wealth while children starve. You can't fix one without fixing all of it.

We can't piecemeal liberation. We can't fight for women and ignore racism; we can't fight for workers and ignore imperialism; we can't fight for Black lives and ignore patriarchy. The system will use one oppression to justify another. All it does is offer some a seat at the table as long as we keep the table intact, and I'm not interested in a seat at the table, I want to break the table.

I believe in something bigger than myself: a world where we support and fight for the liberation of one another unconditionally, because we are human. A world where no one is property, no one is disposable, no one is illegal—and we take care of each other not because we expect a return, but because that's what it means to be free.

That world is possible. But we won't get there by asking nicely. As Frederick Douglass said, "Power concedes nothing without a demand. It never did and it never will." We won't get there by making our movements palatable to the people who benefit from the current one. We won't get there by piecemeal reforms that leave the fundamental logic intact.

We get there by standing on principles. By holding each other accountable. By calling out patriarchy when we see it, racism when we see it, capitalism when we see it—not as separate issues, but as the same fight. We get there by recognizing that until all of us are free, none of us are free.

Until then, the Cornell 7 will keep happening. Bombs will keep falling. The planet will keep burning. And the people at the top will keep telling us that the problem is each other.
